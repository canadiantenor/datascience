Clink is gone, the mountain has opened, and something with a wingspan the width of the valley wants a word. Two routes down, a locked door that opens for a sound, one risky door and one safe one, and thirty pieces of treasure to fight over.

The new thing

Treasure you choose

Last time the treasure sat in a room and you handed it over. This time it works like a card game: after every fight, deal three cards face up and let them keep one. The choosing is the fun. Give them the whole thirty-second argument about which one to take — that argument is the reward.

How it starts

Read aloud

Three weeks of singing lessons. Every evening, on the roof of the bell tower, a small copper dragon and whoever would sit with him, going through the same six notes until he got them right. He got them right on Tuesday.

On Wednesday night the moon went out. Something crossed it — slowly, the way only something enormous can. The bell tower has claw marks in it now, the width of doors. There is a crack in the side of Copperfoot that was not there yesterday, with green light coming out of it.

And Clink is gone.

Then ask"He was learning to sing because of you. What do you say to the people standing around doing nothing?" Let them make the speech. Then go up the hill.
Floor one

The Copper Stair

15 min · a fight either way
Read aloud

Inside the crack is a stair of green copper going down, and down, and down. Nothing is lighting it. It is lit anyway.

At the bottom are two doors. The left one is rattling — something behind it is ticking, fast, like a hundred tiny clocks that are late. The right one is warm to touch, and smells, unmistakably, of soup.

Left — The Rattling Gallery

Three Tick-Tock Beetles made of clockwork and bad temper, guarding a gallery of copper statues.

Reward: deal 3 Common, keep 1

Right — The Ember Kitchen

The Ember Kettle, a stove that developed opinions a century ago and has been holding on to them since.

Reward: deal 3 Common, keep 1

  • Let them pick the door, not you. They will want to know what's behind the other one — tell them afterwards. Wanting the other door is how you get a session three.
  • Both fights have a shortcut written on the monster card. The beetles can be stopped by turning the key on their backs backwards. The Kettle goes out if somebody empties the water jug on it. Both are on the shelf, in plain sight. Mention the jug twice.
Floor two

The Hall of Keys

15 min · puzzle, then a chest
Read aloud

A door of solid green copper, forty feet tall, with no handle, no keyhole and no hinges. Set into it are three stone mouths, open, as though all three are about to say something.

Carved above them, left to right: a mouse. A dragon. A kettle.

  • The door opens for sounds, not keys. They have to make the three noises, out loud, in the order carved: a squeak, a roar, a whistle. No dice. Yes, they have to actually do it. Yes, so do you.
  • Wrong order: all three mouths blow a raspberry and the party is covered in a century of dust. No damage. Try again, as often as they like.
  • Hints, in order: (1) "Read the carvings out loud again." (2) Tam squeaks by accident and the first mouth lights up green. (3) Tam says it: "It doesn't want keys. It wants us to be them."
Then, behind the door

A room the size of a church, and in the middle of it, an open chest with gold running out of it and down the steps like water. Goblets. Rings. A crown with one stone missing. All of it still, and shining, and lit by nothing at all.

ChestDeal 3 Rare cards face up. They keep one. Let them hold all three first.
Floor three

The Fork

20 min · the real decision
Read aloud

Two ways on. Straight ahead, a door of bronze thirty feet high, and sitting in front of it with its hands on its knees, asleep, a bronze giant. Through the gap beneath the door you can see something glittering. A lot of something.

To the left, at the bottom of the wall, a door the size of a shoebox with warm light under it, and the smell of toast.

The Bronze Warden

An elite fight — AC 14, 8 hit points, and it hits two heroes at once. The hardest thing in the adventure.

Win: deal 2 Legendary, keep 1

Or talk it down — say Verdigris's name, or show it Clink's drawing — and walk past safely for 3 Rare, keep 1. Clever is worth less than brave here, and that is the point.

The Mouse Door

Tam's enormous family, forty of them, all talking at once, all insisting you eat something.

Everyone heals to full · deal 2 Common, keep both

No fight, no risk, and the party arrives at the boss with full hearts.

  • Say the trade out loud before they choose. "The big one has the best treasure in the mountain behind it. The little one has breakfast and no fighting. You can't do both." Then be quiet and let them argue.
  • Whatever they pick was right. If they take the safe door, the boss fight goes smoothly and they feel clever. If they take the Warden, the boss fight is a near thing and they feel heroic. Both are good evenings.
Floor four

The Singing Deep

25 min · the boss
Read aloud

The bottom of Copperfoot is not a cave. It is a hall, and it is full of gold — hills of it, slopes of it, a hundred years of coins and cups and crowns heaped up to the ceiling, and every single piece of it polished.

Lying across the top of the biggest heap, with her chin on her claws and her wings folded like a roof, is a copper dragon the size of the bell tower. Her eyes are gold. She has been watching the door since you came in.

Curled up against her side, very small, and not looking at you, is Clink.

"You," says Verdigris, "taught my son to perform. For humans. On a roof."

  • She is not evil and she is not lying. She saw her child standing on a tower in front of a crowd of humans and she drew the obvious conclusion. She has been wrong for three weeks and absolutely certain for all of them.
  • The way to win is Clink singing, and Clink will not sing, because his mother is right there. Coaxing him is the actual boss fight: ask him, sing with him (Corvin's Harmony), say something kind (Mara's Healing Word works on feelings too), or just tell Verdigris to listen. Roll d20 against 10, with advantage if a hero sings with him rather than at him.
  • If they'd rather fight: 14 hit points, and at 7 she stops to breathe — that pause is when somebody can finally be heard. The fight is never the losing path, it is just the longer road to the same door.
  • When he sings: stop. Describe the hall going quiet. She has never heard him. She did not know he could. Let that sit for a second before anybody rolls anything again.
Read aloud at the end

Verdigris puts her head down on the gold, very slowly, so that her eye is level with yours. Up close it is the size of a window.

"Teach him the rest of it," she says. "I will come and listen. From the hill. Where I do not frighten anybody."

Then she reaches up, works one green scale loose from her own throat, and drops it into your hands. It is still warm.

Boss relicHand over Verdigris's Scale. Once per adventure, forever, they can call a dragon for one turn. Put it in the relic row and leave it there.
